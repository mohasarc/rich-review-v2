# What the finished experiments still need

The corpus makes ownership and evidence inspectable. Its open problem is making the consequential choices understandable together without asking the human to consume another large inventory. Preserve the experiments that let a reader predict a consequence, and use those predictions to test whether the explanation has done its job.

This refresh covers **42 finished experiments: 01–42**, at **2026-09-13 04:10:54 UTC**. All were read; the [experiment ledger](experiment-ledger.md) accounts for each. The 38 prototype entry pages, 39's critique page, and six companion pages were opened locally, with additional subject views and selected mechanisms inspected. [Reading scope and limits](review-notes.md) distinguish that work from an exhaustive source audit or a human learning study. Subjects assigned to this critique: none. No page was built.

<a id="stopping"></a>
**A. Moving the long middle upward does not resolve it.**

[01](../01-unconstrained-a/index.html) explicitly makes its full decision sheet the stack's top. [14](../14-outsider-persona/index.html) first teaches the product, then gives the outsider the extensive stack inventory. [21](../21-narrated-top/index.html) needs its decision map after the 90-second film. [22](../22-game-any/game.html) places the complete briefing before its linear game route, with an early jump for readers who want to play immediately. These are honest attempts to preserve completeness. They also move much of the reader's work into the stopping layer.

The distinction matters: **a long top is a reading-cost problem; an unannounced decision is a rule-4 problem.** Rule 4 permits multiple top layers and specifies no viewport budget. I would not fail an artifact because it takes several screens. Conversely, a small topic label does not announce the decision inside that topic.

The concrete witness is [07](../07-stack-timeline/index.html). Its initial #148 summary names “existing idle timing”; the opening mentions deferred idle changes. Selecting #148 supplies construction and navigation acceptance as the events that own the clock, and names readiness/completion resets as deferred alternatives. Those events determine whether a long command consumes the idle interval. They are substantive choices, not merely the names of implementing functions. This reproduces 41's finding in a [fresh capture](receipts/07-idle-descent.json). The repair is to state the event rule in its parent. A bigger source archive would leave the omission intact.

The late reader checks add distinct witnesses. [42](../42-fresh-reader-refresh-10/review.md) finds optional host policy first appearing below the complete layers in 12/23/29, and the qualification that a removed timeout was unused appearing below 25's pocket brief. [39](../39-fresh-reader-check/evidence.html#r4-fixtures) flags 27's loss of a storage premise; 42 classifies its concrete disk-cleanup miss as an omission rather than an observed descent surprise. These are their reported routes, not additional descents I independently reproduced. The disagreement is useful: retain the exact parent, child, and proposition at issue instead of counting flags or treating an unflagged artifact as complete.

[13](../13-owner-persona/index.html) offers a useful opposite example. Its collapsed fixture row already names inline-zero becoming 64 KiB, a precreated directory, and an unused memory option. Opening it supplies the particular cleanup fixture and translation formulas. The row is compact but carries the consequence-bearing inputs. [23's opening](receipts/23-zoom-canvas-opening.png) demonstrates a different possibility: dense spatial grouping can keep many choices visible together. Its rationale problems below remain separate from that layout experiment. [34](../34-inverted-for-experts/index.html) compresses through a maintainer's recognition of evidence. These are candidates for comparison using **one reconciled set of propositions**, rather than three different inventories.

<a id="reasons"></a>
**B. “Nobody said” is valuable enough to require a precise search boundary.**

The statement that a reason was not found is an observation about the explainer's search. A tag cannot turn it into a fact about every author conversation. Several builders say this explicitly; the actual records need to maintain the same precision.

It separately explains clearing entries before project release. | Why changed completion/rejection visibility fits the broader preservation promise remains a different question. 04's “PR body silent” opening needs that qualification at the point of appearance. 31/33/37 make the distinction especially explicit. |

This is why different counts of “unexplained decisions” cannot be compared as coverage or suspicion. One artifact splits clearing order, completion propagation, and the preservation tension; another places them on a single stated card with an unexplained clause. The underlying propositions may be substantially the same.

The useful reusable unit is already suggested by [31's runbook](../31-method-runbook/RUNBOOK.md): **choice → consequence → specific reason or bounded absence → evidence → the parent sentence that announces it**. Keep the source that discloses a choice separate from the source that explains it. Keep the explanation of a test's new home separate from the reason for removing a particular observation. This improves attribution without requiring a uniform page design.

**C. Preserve representations that expose a relationship, and keep their interpretations synchronized.**

The recurring sequence is ownership picture → visible register → mechanism → source drawer → file inventory. The repetition is more consequential than the common use of HTML. Changing “decision” to “clue,” “move,” or “memory” often leaves the reader performing the same scan-and-expand task. Multiple representations earn their space when they change what the reader can see or do.

These paths are preserved in [model-routes.json](receipts/model-routes.json). They support carrying forward specific interactions, without choosing a winning artifact.

The late synthesis [40](../40-best-of-synthesis/index.html) gives reuse a coherent purpose. Its [opening](receipts/40-best-of-synthesis-opening.png) asks whether empty caches mean release has finished; its request tour, paired identity matrices, and held-cleanup recordings address different parts of that question. The PR's disclosure and its missing parity reconciliation remain separate. Credits and original recordings make borrowing inspectable. Requiring a contribution from thirteen predecessors still produces a long lesson with an optional navigation matrix its own author would drop. Carry forward the causal composition and scoped provenance; the synthesis does not establish a preferred page grammar or a comprehension benefit.

The fresh issue in 36 illustrates how an otherwise strong picture can lose precision. With **At the service return** and **R** selected, the matrix correctly becomes diagonal and the selected pair reports different projection objects. Yet the plot remains named “Reference locations,” and the captions still say “One large block” and “Two smaller blocks.” The [captured view](receipts/36-reference-return.png) shows the mismatch. Those captions describe the cache view; the underlying cache claim can remain true while the displayed relation has changed. Rename the plotted object and update or explicitly qualify the captions with the selected lens. This is a criticism of the explanation's units, not symnav correctness.

The collection should carry that requirement into any reusable visualization: changing the observed boundary must change the names and interpretation that explain it. Correct data and a working toggle are insufficient by themselves.

<a id="judgment"></a>
**D. Explaining the move should get the reader to a meaningful alternative.**

Much of the corpus accurately answers “where did this responsibility go?” and “what did the spec request?” The philosophy also asks the human to recognize an undesirable choice. That needs an account of what the boundary commits the system to, and what additional context could make another choice preferable.

[11](../11-chess-coach/index.html) is strongest at the fixture fork: normalize old tiny limits, or preserve a witnessed spill using a different valid input. The physical path makes the alternative concrete. Its generic questions about recomputing an already-made decision are less demanding; they tend to invite agreement with the presented move. [16](../16-drag-the-box/index.html) makes a counterfactual tangible by carrying dependencies with a module. [29](../29-refusal/index.html) separates four questions that could receive different human answers. [35](../35-future-self/index.html) asks what the returning author is about to change, making a decision useful at the moment it becomes relevant.

A useful next artifact would trace **which decisions constrain another decision**, using one explicitly hypothetical requirement on these same subjects—for example, a host that needs a different output-storage policy. Show which existing contracts it touches and where the source supplies no rationale. The human would supply the product constraint and discuss the alternatives. That is an editorial proposal, not a recovered implementing-author intention or a new symnav change request.

<a id="evidence"></a>
**E. Keep the useful negative results; move audit bulk below the explanation.**

[19](../19-type-level-only/README.md) explicitly cannot satisfy rule 8 while refusing to inspect bodies and assertions. Its declaration view usefully separates requiredness, structural assignability, and provenance. The notice about unseen changes does not surface the missing changes themselves. It belongs as a partial lens or negative result; a complete review would need an independently attributed implementation/test account.

[28](../28-blast-radius-tool/README.md) retains a policy-field query that missed known consumers and explains its replacement seeds. It also exposes measured-zero error provenance and unmeasured test decisions. That makes the measure inspectable. Direct reference reach still cannot decide which architectural choice deserves human attention. [27](../27-test-honesty/index.html) gives the more general lesson: trace setup, exercised code, assertion, and observation boundary. A migrated title and a passing total cannot substitute for that explanation.

The new reader checks show why the evidence layer must be capable of correcting the explanation. [42's pinned source comparison](../42-fresh-reader-refresh-10/source-check/evidence.md#s1) supports last-configured primary ownership, contradicting the first-owner wording in 01/22; its next finding rejects 01's claimed edit to an old #127 test. Both 39 and 42 repeatedly fail to learn #131's validation-before-worker-construction consequence, while learning it from 20's contract account. This is a useful change in explanatory precision: a dependency arriving at a constructor and a failure moving before thread creation are different propositions. These are source/recall findings from those critics, not new symnav executions by this one.

[17](../17-executable-before-after/index.html) gives actual execution a carefully bounded role. Controlled scheduling exposes the promise contract; matching query results describe the exercised fixtures. I inspected its recordings and interface, without restarting its build-and-run server. The same distinction applies to other builders' reported test runs throughout this critique.

Local snapshots, revision-pinned excerpts, and return links are substantial achievements. Their marginal value is greatest at evidence depth. Many builders independently nominate their complete file census, duplicated excerpts, or generic mechanism strip for removal. Keep the evidence accessible; stop making scope accounting part of every reader's linear lesson. Likewise, [31](../31-method-runbook/RUNBOOK.md) and [32](../32-kit/KIT.md) correctly limit their validators to structural relationships. Assigning every diff line or matching every claim ID does not prove that the prose above it preserves the claim.

<a id="learning"></a>
**F. The next missing observation comes from a reader.**

No finished material in this census reports an unfamiliar-human comprehension result. The builders have tested code, models, links, layout, and their own explanations extensively. These critiques have read the same facts repeatedly. Neither activity establishes that a human acquired a usable model from one artifact.

There is now an intermediate result worth preserving. [39](../39-fresh-reader-check/README.md) and [42](../42-fresh-reader-refresh-10/README.md) seal page-derived recall before their source pass and preserve corrections afterward. They distinguish a reader miss, a page omission, a false claim, and a demonstrated surprise. Their explicit serial-reading contamination prevents a comparison of opening effectiveness, but it does not erase the concrete diagnostic evidence. Future critiques should start from those witnesses and record what changed, rather than repeat that only interface checks exist.

Owner and outsider experiments also remain separate presentations. [13](../13-owner-persona/index.html) economizes on familiar context; [14](../14-outsider-persona/index.html) establishes what the product does; [34](../34-inverted-for-experts/index.html) starts from recognizable evidence. The philosophy's stronger promise is that readers can choose their needed depth within the same account. Navigation is present; reader fit remains unobserved.

The existing corpus supports three bounded follow-ups:

| Follow-up | Hold fixed | Observe without a leaderboard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compare a compact surface with a full register | Reconciled choices, consequential qualifications, reason scope, and evidence exits | Ask the reader to explain the boundary before descent; record the exact proposition that surprises them below. Repair its parent. |

Keep misconceptions and reasoning, rather than treating successful clicks as learning. Reading both openings in succession carries knowledge over. |
| Reuse [31's method](../31-method-runbook/RUNBOOK.md) or [32's kit](../32-kit/KIT.md) on a wider assigned subject | The procedure or unmodified components | Record where extraction, rationale scope, geometry, and navigation cease to fit. Rebuilding the same authored #127 ledger does not test transfer. |

The philosophy's eventual reader response remains deliberately outside this read-only round. The absence of approve/reject or stored comments is compliance with rule 2. Temporary model actions, sketch marks, and fixture inputs can support learning without collecting a review. The next work should preserve that boundary while testing whether the human has something informed to say.
