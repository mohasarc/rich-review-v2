# What the finished experiments have established

The experiments make source-backed ownership explanations credible. They have not yet established the philosophy's central claim: an unfamiliar human can understand a change well enough to question a decision. The largest unresolved design problem is the repeated use of a long decision register as the complete stopping layer.

This is a critique of the explanations, not a correctness review of symnav. The scope and reading depth are recorded in [review notes](review-notes.md); every finished experiment has an individual entry in the [experiment ledger](experiment-ledger.md). Earlier experiments were read. No page was built.

## The complete critique, before the supporting detail

| Finding | What is missing or repeating | What is promising, and what to do with it |

An approximate map of the collection's evidence boundary:

    pinned sources → recovered decisions → working explanation → reader's usable understanding
         checked        authored + audited       checked           not yet observed

The last gap is about what the reader can explain and challenge. It is not a request for approve/reject controls or stored comments. The philosophy's eventual response product is deliberately outside this round's read-only contract.

## F1. What the checks actually establish

The reports are clear about bounded execution. **[10](experiment-ledger.md#e10)** replays a captured semantic request; **[17](experiment-ledger.md#e17)** can run actual compiled backends with editable fixtures through an optional server; **[15](experiment-ledger.md#e15)** compares an illustrative model with real transport probes against a scripted peer. These are substantive attempts to keep explanation answerable to the code. They do not show that a person acquired a usable model.

The same separation applies to validation of the explanation. A source hash establishes the identity of the cited snapshot. A browser check establishes that an exit opens and returns. A claim-ID check establishes that an authored identifier has a parent. None establishes that the parent tells the reader enough, that the diagram is easier than its paragraph, or that the reader could reject a poor architectural alternative. **[31](experiment-ledger.md#e31)** and **[32](experiment-ledger.md#e32)** explicitly acknowledge this limit. Their honesty should become the collection's default interpretation of validation.

The recurring “What I would do next” is a fresh-reader exercise. That recurrence identifies unfinished research, not a defect in every builder. It would be misleading to name a winning explanation from these artifacts alone.

**[33](experiment-ledger.md#e33)** is valuable because it holds the common body, geometry, and evidence constant. Its matched openings establish an inspectable comparison. They do not establish that metaphor or motivating failure teaches better. Likewise, builder-created sketches in **[25](experiment-ledger.md#e25)** are demonstrations of the editor, not reader data.

## F2. Completeness is being paid for with reading

Rule 4 allows multiple top layers; it does not prescribe a viewport or word count. Nevertheless, the philosophy says a person can grasp the top, judge its decisions, and stop. Several artifacts explicitly record how expensive their solution became:

| Artifact | Declared complete surface | Consequence for this critique |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **[01](experiment-ledger.md#e01)** | Map plus 141 recovered decisions, with policy records and deadline absences | The six entrances orient; they do not replace the long inventory. |
| **[03](experiment-ledger.md#e03)** | Picture plus 24 choices | An honest account of the cost of a complete opening for one large PR. |
| **[14](experiment-ledger.md#e14)** | Orientation plus the complete choice sheet | A newcomer first learns the product and then faces the extensive stack inventory. |
| **[21](experiment-ledger.md#e21)** | Film plus visible decision map | Ninety seconds measures the orientation, not the complete stopping layer. |
| **[22](experiment-ledger.md#e22)** | Maps, game contracts, 117 PR choices, 20 supplementary observations, policy rows, and absent deadlines | The game is available by an early jump, but the linear route crosses a large briefing. |
| **13 / 23** | Compact consequence register / six-room outline | Completeness need not always mean spacious cards across many screens. Comprehension remains an open question. |

These are not comparable decision counts: some are families, some are individual choices, and some include inherited context. They are evidence of different editorial units and acknowledged reading costs.

Calling the whole register “top” protects against one kind of omission. It does not by itself build the pyramid imagined by the philosophy. The opposite shortcut is equally weak: “tests changed” does not tell a reader that a particular integration observation disappeared. A parent must carry the consequential qualification, even when source names and exact mechanics remain below it.

The next compression experiment should hold decision propositions and rationale gaps constant, then change their arrangement. The author would need to show what each compressed parent actually says, not merely that it shares an ID with a detail. **31's runbook** states this distinction well; **32's layer validator** cannot enforce it semantically.

Inherited context also needs an explicit editorial rule. The unchanged policy schema, its 39 numeric fields, and a policy record with 44 values/recipes are different inventories. Reprinting the latter is not evidence that a page found 44 additional changes. A number that materially determines the human's choice belongs at the stopping layer; supporting precision can deepen an already announced contract. The collection has not settled that boundary consistently.

## F3. The repetition is in the reading task

The common pattern is stronger than the shared choice of HTML:

    orientation → complete decision register → optional mechanism → local source → file inventory

**[08](experiment-ledger.md#e08)** adds a worst honest reading and counterevidence. **[11](experiment-ledger.md#e11)** adds alternatives. **[12](experiment-ledger.md#e12)** adds clues. **[26](experiment-ledger.md#e26)** adds a physical cabinet. **[35](experiment-ledger.md#e35)** adds a returning author's cues. Those differences can matter, but much of the work still asks the reader to scan a set of authored records and choose one to expand.

The READMEs repeatedly nominate the same things for removal: full file censuses, repeated source excerpts, generic before/after panels, autoplay, and introductory analogies that the real diagram makes redundant. Examples include **[02](experiment-ledger.md#e02)/[03](experiment-ledger.md#e03)/[05](experiment-ledger.md#e05)/[06](experiment-ledger.md#e06)/[08](experiment-ledger.md#e08)/[12](experiment-ledger.md#e12)/[13](experiment-ledger.md#e13)** on inventories, **[07](experiment-ledger.md#e07)/[10](experiment-ledger.md#e10)** on autoplay, and **[30](experiment-ledger.md#e30)/[31](experiment-ledger.md#e31)/[33](experiment-ledger.md#e33)/[34](experiment-ledger.md#e34)** on repeated explanatory panels. This is strong agreement across otherwise independent attempts.

That does not make the repeated investigation waste. Independent source reads repeatedly corrected tempting stories: policy already crossed the boundary before #131; #148 stages an implementation before its production consumer switches; clearing a cache is distinct from waiting for cleanup. Agreement on those distinctions is useful. Agreement on a page shell is not evidence that the shell teaches best.

Keep local evidence access, but let an ordinary reader encounter file lists when they need to audit scope. Keep the label that distinguishes an illustration from execution. Move builder/harness details and generic statements about forbidden UI controls into the handoff unless they help the reader use an interaction. “Reload clears this sketch” has a practical purpose that “this is a read-only experiment” often lacks.

The open-book game in **[22](experiment-ledger.md#e22)** and optional sketch comparison in **[25](experiment-ledger.md#e25)** reveal a real constraint: learning activities cannot gate facts that the hard rules require up front. Both preserve access. That is a useful design discovery, even though it reduces the surprise or recall challenge those activities might otherwise use.

## F4. Rationale is an evidence problem before it is a badge

There is a concrete disagreement worth resolving.

**23, D03** says no reason for the timing of policy-testing removal was found in the PR, commits, or plans. At the same #148 head, the policy record's “Migration access” section explicitly schedules removal of that subpath after app-owned mechanism tests move package-local. **[06](experiment-ledger.md#e06)** consulted this record and revised its initial attribution. The pinned passage is copied in [the rationale check](receipts/rationale-check.md).

The repair is more specific than flipping an entire compound record to stated. Separate the planned subpath retirement from the rationale for particular removed checks or changed observations. The migration passage supplies the former. It does not establish equivalence or motivation for every test alteration. An unexplained badge should identify a particular unresolved choice and the search corpus that produced it.

The same granularity issue appears around #127. Its PR body explicitly names the backend's awaited release boundary and changes the public release signature. It also gives a reason for clearing caches before project release. Those are different facts from a reason for changing the caller's completion/error contract under the broader preservation requirement.

**04's detailed card** acknowledges the disclosed API while retaining a missing-rationale finding; its README's “Neither PR body mentions it” is too easy to read as a broader claim. **[09](experiment-ledger.md#e09)** separates plan horizon from PR disclosure. **[17](experiment-ledger.md#e17)** groups synchronous clearing and joining release under a stated card. **[24](experiment-ledger.md#e24)** distinguishes the stated barrier from the missing reconciliation with parity. The useful disagreement concerns exactly what proposition has a reason. It should not be flattened into “the author never mentioned release.”

Similarly, **[03](experiment-ledger.md#e03)** treats portable CRLF normalization as stated, whereas **23, D22** asks for a further reason for the digest mechanism. A reason to normalize line endings and a reason to choose a particular freeze mechanism deserve separate treatment when the status differs.

A reusable record should preserve:

| Field | Why it is needed |
| --- | --- |
| Exact choice and before/after consequence | Prevents a family heading from standing in for several independent decisions. |
| Where the choice is disclosed | A disclosed choice can lack a rationale; a retrospective body is not an original request. |
| Specific reason, or bounded unsuccessful search | Prevents a general architecture goal from explaining every implementation detail by implication. |
| Implementation/test evidence and its scope | Separates what runs, what is asserted, and what a probe actually observed. |
| Parent statement | Makes a semantic descent audit possible; matching identifiers alone is insufficient. |

Different counts are not automatic evidence of missing decisions. They are a reason to build a crosswalk. The **[31](experiment-ledger.md#e31)** ledger is a starting point for that comparison: standardize the evidence relationship while leaving room for different explanations.

## F5. What earns a picture or an interaction

The strongest representations let the reader make a consequential distinction. Their suitability depends on the question; 36's documented need for a separate completion readout is useful evidence of a picture's limit.

| Representation to preserve | What it makes available to the reader | Practical limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **[05](experiment-ledger.md#e05)** package and runtime maps | A policy package is not a service; an existing process bridge can acquire new consumers without moving those consumers. | More graph detail can become another reading burden. |
| **[03](experiment-ledger.md#e03)/[06](experiment-ledger.md#e06)/[07](experiment-ledger.md#e07)/[18](experiment-ledger.md#e18)** active and staged graphs | New source ownership does not mean the shipped CLI has adopted that implementation. | Import maps and copied-source equality do not establish runtime equivalence. |
| **[10](experiment-ledger.md#e10)/[17](experiment-ledger.md#e17)** captured or executable cache boundaries | Identical query results can coexist with changed release completion; clearing, retaining a promise, and refilling a handle are distinct. | Controlled collaborators and omitted daemon/CLI paths must stay visible. I inspected recordings, not a fresh execution of symnav. |
| **[36](experiment-ledger.md#e36)** pairwise identity matrices | The same retained location can feed fresh service-return projections. A matrix shows relationships across observations at once. | Identity is compared within a build. Matching matrices miss the changed completion contract, so the author supplies a separate release-state readout. This is a one-file controlled sample. |
| **[11](experiment-ledger.md#e11)/[27](experiment-ledger.md#e27)/[34](experiment-ledger.md#e34)** stimulus, exercised path, assertion | An empty directory can follow “a spill file was cleaned” or “no spill file was created.” Timeout selection depends on caller purpose. | This should support a question about the chosen contract, not become a code-correctness verdict. |
| **[16](experiment-ledger.md#e16)** moving a real module's dependencies | Imagining repaired addresses does not remove a prohibited type dependency. | The board moves a whole module and computes a bounded static model; it is not a compiler result. |
| **[22](experiment-ledger.md#e22)/[30](experiment-ledger.md#e30)** independently controlled lifetime gates | Receiving bytes, settling append, finishing a delivery, acknowledging a result, and releasing the next turn are different events. | Events are illustrative. Completing a walkthrough can still be button following rather than understanding. |
| **[25](experiment-ledger.md#e25)/[26](experiment-ledger.md#e26)** reconstruction and bounded analogy | A reader can express ownership; an old ticket/promise can survive an emptied drawer. | A supplied label palette cues the answer; an analogy needs its failure boundary. |

I exercised **16's** lifetime move with imagined address repairs: the telemetry type dependency remained. In **[30](experiment-ledger.md#e30)**, settling the first attachment left the next request waiting; settling the latest attachment and the resource sample allowed it to start while acknowledgement remained outstanding. These are observations of the teaching models, not new symnav test results.

Two narrower methods deserve explicit limits. **[19](experiment-ledger.md#e19)** says it cannot satisfy rule 8's demand for all implementation and weakened/deleted-assertion decisions while refusing to read bodies. That is an acknowledged hard-rule shortfall, not a successful exemption. Its requiredness and assignability distinctions remain useful as a subordinate view or documented negative result.

**[28](experiment-ledger.md#e28)** records that a policy-field reference query misses known consumer accesses, then measures selected consumer symbols instead. It also reports that eight of nine nonzero production footprints have unchanged file sets. The final tool is inspectable, but “widest reach first” remains an editorial priority based on a partial static proxy. Its zero-radius error-provenance choice and unmeasured test decisions are reasons not to equate that ordering with importance.

## F6. Several entrances, still one untested reader

The philosophy asks for the same document to support a codebase owner and someone new to the area. Separate persona experiments do not yet establish that property.

**[14](experiment-ledger.md#e14)** does something many artifacts assume away: it explains what symnav is for before explaining daemon architecture. **[13](experiment-ledger.md#e13)** makes an owner's first useful action inspecting a consequence. **[34](experiment-ledger.md#e34)** begins with discriminating test inputs and allows abstraction on demand. This is an alternative to forcing every expert through the same orientation sequence. It still preserves an early ownership picture and a complete decision surface.

**[24](experiment-ledger.md#e24)** gives the reader meaningful questions and remembers the route back. Its linear mode preserves access to the same material. That is a more useful form of nonlinear reading than a collection of generic “learn more” buttons. The remaining question is whether the choices match what readers actually wonder.

**[35](experiment-ledger.md#e35)** adds another entrance: “What are you about to change?” Its cues tie likely future edits to a recorded decision and pinned historical state. This helps resist remembering an intermediate staging PR as the completed migration. It has not observed six-month recall or a returning author's edit task.

**[37](experiment-ledger.md#e37)** makes decision and depth independent coordinates. A reader can compare cache clearing with release completion while staying at Mechanism depth. This is a specific addition to reader control. The repeated selected summary costs space, and the author reports reduced spatial benefit on a phone. Comparing it with ordinary tabs using identical content would show whether the independent axes earn their place.

Multiple representations should give the reader alternative ways to acquire the same model. They should not silently become a checklist of formats the reader must finish. The current documents offer many exits; the reader's ability to stop confidently still needs observation.

## F7. The next experiments should resolve specific uncertainties

These are proposals from this critique. They are not implementing-author intentions, new symnav work, or requests to add response collection to the artifacts.

| Next trial | Hold fixed | Observe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Use 33 with unfamiliar readers.** Assign an opening per reader; use a counterbalanced follow-up only with attention to carryover. | Common body, facts, source, layout, task. | Can the reader distinguish scope ownership, cache clearing, and release completion, and identify the unresolved rationale? Preference alone is insufficient. |
| **Compare a compact stopping layer with a full register.** Use one reconciled ledger, informed by 13 and 23. | Every decision proposition, consequential qualification, reason status, and evidence exit. | What can readers predict from the top? Which deeper facts surprise them? Record the time required rather than assuming a short page is faster. |
| **Test transfer with 15, 25, or 30.** Change a caller purpose, ask the reader to explain their arrows, or settle attachments in an unfamiliar order. | The relevant source-derived contract and available top-level facts. | Does the reader explain the controlling scope or repeat labels and the demonstrated sequence? Ask for a reasoned tradeoff or design question as well as the predicted event. |
| **Cross-check 31's extraction and reuse 32 beyond the small demo.** Use another operator or a later separate run on a wider assigned subject. | Frozen procedure or component directory; pinned inputs. | Which choices or rationale attributions disagree, where the layout fails, and which work still requires editorial judgment. Rebuilding the same authored ledger is not this test. |
| **Try 35 on a concrete return task.** Start from an intended future edit. | Historical snapshot and source links. | Can a returning reader recover the relevant constraint and its reason without retaking the whole tour? A present-day lookup trial would test retrieval, not long-term retention. |

The immediate carry-forward set is small: exact evidence and return links; separate package, process, and lifetime boundaries; explicit active-versus-staged state; causal examples with disclosed limits; specific rationale gaps; and question-based entrances. Keep the remaining presentation choices open until reader evidence distinguishes them.
