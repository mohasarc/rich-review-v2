# The stopping layer still needs to earn the reader’s trust

The finished experiments demonstrate many ways to expose mechanisms. They have not yet demonstrated the central promise of [philosophy.md](../../philosophy.md): that a reader can understand enough to judge the decisions, choose where to descend, and safely stop. The largest remaining problem is editorial. A complete-looking inventory can still omit a changed failure boundary, conceal a changed test premise, or misstate what the author explained. Adding another representation does not repair that inventory.

This critique reads **all 43 finished experiments, 01–43, at 2026-09-13 04:25:23 UTC**, including the earlier critiques. It produces no page and no ranking. [The ledger](experiment-ledger.md) covers each experiment; [the witnesses](witnesses.md) separate directly checked cases from findings inherited from the reader checks. [The method](review-method.md) states the completion rule and the limits of this reading.

## What the refresh changes

[38](../38-critique/README.md) and [41](../41-critique-refresh-10/README.md) already identify long complete layers, repeated card-and-drawer structures, missing human evidence, and problems with rationale attribution. Repeating their recommendations is not additional validation. This pass adds the finished synthesis, both completed reader reports, direct comparison of several claims with their descendants and sources, and selected interaction checks.

[43](../43-critique-refresh-20/critique.md) finished before this handoff and is included. It shares many of these conclusions, reproduces 07’s idle-event descent, and identifies stale captions in 36’s return-value view. My own captured return state confirms that caption problem. It also usefully narrows 29’s rationale question: a planned export retirement does not answer the reason for an exact enforcement tradeoff. These are attributed additions; another similar critique is not another independent learning result.

[40](../40-best-of-synthesis/index.html) supplies a useful example of synthesis as reconciliation. Its opening places the same zero-entry cache beside a fulfilled old release and a pending new release. That exposes the decision immediately. D08 and D09 then distinguish an explained ordering choice from an unrecorded reconciliation with the plan’s preservation promise. Its identity view also places cached objects and service returns beside each other, making a difference visible that 36 initially asks the reader to discover through a toggle.

The thirteen-donor construction also reveals a limit of accumulation. A contact sheet, request trace, cleanup interaction, analogy, alternative opening, question exits, and depth index do not all need equal prominence. The optional depth index duplicates existing routes, as the author recognizes. Per-figure research credits interrupt the teaching voice; a linked credit table can preserve attribution. The promising result is a repaired claim or a better comparison, not proof that including something from every donor improves understanding.

[39](../39-fresh-reader-check/index.html) and [42](../42-fresh-reader-refresh-10/review.md) change an earlier conclusion: there are now reader records, not merely prompts for future readers. Both write recall before consulting external source and acknowledge serial learning across overlapping pages. Their differing cutoffs and routes matter. Neither covers 40, and neither is a set of independent human trials. Their recurring misses—early parent-side worker validation, fixture casts, or the selected warm-request protocol field—are evidence about what those readings taught. They are not automatically proof that every page lacks the information everywhere.

The reports also disagree usefully. For 27’s adapter explanation, 39 calls the lower qualification a rule-4 surprise; 42 calls the concrete lost disk witness an omission still requiring source inspection. My direct parent/child reading confirms the misleading compression, but does not erase that distinction. The top needs to say that an unchanged-looking fixture can cease to exercise file creation. Calling this R4, R8, or both requires specifying exactly which proposition appears at which layer. A larger pile of flags would obscure that work.

## What is missing

### Completeness at the level of consequences

The stopping layer must account for independent decisions, not just touched objects. “Policy reaches the worker” tells me about supply. It does not tell me that malformed serialized policy can now throw in the parent constructor before a worker exists. “Adapters keep small test knobs” tells me about convenience. It does not tell me that a formerly disk-backed cleanup test now remains inline. “Six Maps become six handles” tells me about ownership. It does not tell me that the caller now waits for a rejecting project-release promise.

This is why the very large contract inventory in [20](../20-contract-table/index.html) remains instructive. Its #131 view teaches the early parent failure particularly well. Its #127 complete layer has five decisions about production contracts and does not give the ten added tests their own decision account. The method’s unit of enumeration—callables—has filtered out a required class of decisions. By comparison, [19](../19-type-level-only/index.html) openly declares that its declaration-only method cannot recover bodies and test behavior. That is a useful negative result; it remains insufficient as the complete PR explanation required by rule 8.

This does not require every source line or identifier at the top. Rule 4 permits additional fidelity; it forbids discovering a consequential qualification below an apparently sufficient parent. A parent needs to carry the condition, the outcome that matters, and the reason or its absence. “Recovery preserves behavior” cannot parent a lower explanation of a different escaping error. “Test helpers changed” cannot parent any imaginable lost witness. Naming the file or theme is not enough.

### An honest account of what is known about reasons

There are three separate questions: **What changes? Where is the change disclosed? Why was it chosen?** A fourth sometimes matters: **How does it fit the stated constraint?** The collection often compresses these into one badge.

[04](../04-textbook-chapter/index.html) says in its opening that the PR body is silent about the release failure path. Its own deeper decision account says the body names the awaited release boundary. The supplied body does name it. The still-missing explanation is why that changed completion/error contract fits the wider preservation requirement. That distinction matters to a human deciding whether to ask about intent, implementation, or an apparent contradiction in the plan.

The retirement of `@symnav/daemon/policy-testing` provides a second pattern. Several #148 pages mark the retirement unexplained, although the pinned policy plan schedules it after tests move package-local. [06](../06-hub-and-spokes/index.html) found that source. The plan does not explain every nearby deleted check, so replacing the whole group’s badge with *stated* would repeat the same error in the other direction. [Concrete sources and bounded corrections](witnesses.md#reason-attribution).

“Nobody said” is valuable only after a proportionate search of the supplied rationale. A raw diff excludes unchanged plans; an assertion count excludes the fixture premise; a public signature excludes a newly awaited promise. Each is useful evidence with an editorial blind spot.

### A path from mechanism to judgment

Most artifacts can now answer “where did this responsibility move?” Fewer let a human weigh what that movement commits them to. A plan citation can establish provenance without establishing a tradeoff. A preserved numeric default can be fully disclosed without saying why it remains a reasonable product choice. Where the inputs are silent, the artifact should expose the exact missing assumption instead of supplying an invented rationale.

The promising beginnings are specific. [11](../11-chess-coach/index.html) contrasts timeout-by-purpose with a simpler alternative. [16](../16-drag-the-box/index.html) lets a module carry its actual imports across a hypothetical boundary, then distinguishes address repairs from a remaining forbidden dependency. [29](../29-refusal/index.html) separates clocks, authority, staging, and the public client into discussable cuts, while stating that these are not verified cherry-pickable patches. [35](../35-future-self/index.html) creates routes back from future edits to historical choices, but does not establish six-month retention merely by naming that persona.

A useful next task would ask a reader to apply one of these already explained decisions to a clearly hypothetical requirement: for example, a caller needs a different policy, or cleanup must remain pending while a request is retained. The reader should identify which announced boundary constrains that requirement, what alternative is available, and which rationale is absent. This tests understanding useful to judgment without collecting a verdict or pretending the agent knows the product’s future. Decision-dependency views were already suggested by 41; they remain an untested direction here.

### A reader who has not already learned the subject all night

39 and 42 are valuable precisely because their limitations are recorded. They distinguish source correction, recall miss, and descent surprise. They also read many variants of the same PR in sequence. Once a reader has learned the awaited release distinction, recognizing it on the next page cannot measure that next page’s teaching power.

[33](../33-pair-one-variable/README.md) controls opening length and the shared body, but no independent-reader comparison establishes which opening works. [22](../22-game-any/README.md) and [25](../25-reader-sketch/README.md) make teach-back possible, but shipping the activity is not evidence that someone learned. [31](../31-method-runbook/README.md) demonstrates one execution of a documented method, not reproducibility across unfamiliar subjects and operators.

The next useful evidence is a small number of separate readers, each encountering a subject once, with an unaided account recorded before source or hints. Ask what changed, what remains possible, what reason was supplied, and what choice they would need human context to weigh. Record actual omissions and later revisions to the account. Keep this qualitative: no leaderboard, hidden gold answer set, or correctness verdict controls. The experiment can record the research observation outside the review artifact without adding comment storage to it.

## What repeats

The predominant grammar is stable: a compact picture, a large “complete” decision list, repeated decision titles in mechanisms, a local source viewer, and audit inventories. The assigned metaphors and personas vary more than this reading path does. A workshop, cabinet, or ticket changes the vocabulary; it becomes a different explanation only if it makes a relationship easier to see or a useful prediction possible.

This is not a claim that every long page fails, or that the first viewport must contain every choice. Rule 4 explicitly permits multiple top layers. [23](../23-zoom-canvas/index.html) fits its six-room, 25-choice overview on a desktop canvas; it should not be described as another long scroll just because its text extraction is long. [21](../21-narrated-top/README.md) labels its ninety-second film as orientation and points to a complete map. [32’s starter](../32-kit/starter.html) identifies itself as one decision and links the full demo. These declared fragments are different from a complete layer that silently omits a consequential input.

The recurring cost is rereading the same middle. [37](../37-be-weird-b/index.html) separates decision and depth navigation, yet a selected depth pane can repeat the already visible summary and reason. In 40, a second navigation system does not add a second explanatory relationship. In 07 and 10, autoplay makes an author’s timing part of the reader’s work; both authors question it. In 04, a worked example earns its details more clearly than each further rendering earns repetition.

Repeated interpretation can also become misleading when the picture changes. In 36’s reference-return lens the matrix becomes diagonal, correctly depicting fresh projections, while its plot title still names reference locations and its captions still describe a large block and two smaller blocks. Those describe the stored-object view. Update the title and interpretation with the lens, or explicitly tie the old captions to the cached objects. A working toggle with correct data does not by itself preserve the picture’s meaning. [Witness and captured state](witnesses.md#changing-view).

Source mirrors, pinned hashes, changed-file inventories, local execution records, and browser checks make claims inspectable. Keep those receipts. They belong behind a concrete question unless they themselves teach the decision. “All hunks assigned” is an audit of assignment. “Every local page opened” is an opening check. Neither establishes that a person can stop with a correct mental model.

Before synthesizing them further, repair their concrete claims and test the act of stopping. The morning decision is which learning operation each device earns, not which page contains the most devices.
