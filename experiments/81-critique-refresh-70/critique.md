# The pictures are starting to carry the explanation

The new experiments change the diagnosis. It is no longer fair to describe this collection simply as decision cards with different decorations. Several recent pages make a relationship understandable through the representation itself: an old promise stays in the reader's hand after a lookup is cleared; an early return leaves later checks visibly unvisited; a policy field remains the same thread through several consumers. Those are advances toward the understanding described in [philosophy.md](../../philosophy.md).

The missing step is connecting that local understanding to an informed judgment about a change. The collection has many ways to demonstrate what happens, substantial machinery for checking what was said, and much less evidence that an unfamiliar person can explain why the design was chosen or identify circumstances in which it should be challenged. Completeness also remains expensive: the interesting instrument often sits above another long register that carries the actual review obligations.

This critique covers **all 78 finished handoffs at 2026-09-13 11:52:58 UTC**: experiments 01–78, including the interrupted negative results 56, 58, and 60. Experiments 79, 80, and 82 had no README at the cutoff. I read earlier experiments and critiques; this is a cumulative inspection by a primed reader, not another fresh-reader trial. The [ledger](experiment-ledger.md) accounts for every handoff. [Inspection notes](inspection-notes.md) distinguish reading, browser operations, authors' reported evidence, and my three direct source checks.

## What the latest briefs changed

The [61 brief](../61-pixel-causality-grid/brief.md), like the recent representation briefs, explicitly permits teaching one narrow aspect without explaining the whole PR or stack. That changes the denominator. A narrow lesson is not a failed whole-stack inventory. Its deeper states still owe the reader the same facts at greater fidelity, but unrelated decisions outside its declared boundary are not automatically missing parents.

This matters especially for 68. Its control flow lesson can be small because it declares what it teaches. Its success does not establish that a complete PR can be made equally small. Conversely, demanding every #131 test adaptation in that microscope would erase the experiment it was asked to run. The unresolved campaign problem is **composition across these valid small lessons**, not uniform noncompliance by the new batch. [Playbook §1](../../playbook.md) requires completeness; the briefs define the scope within which to assess it.

I also distinguish a complete stopping layer from a first viewport. Rule 4 does not prohibit scrolling. A long root may obey that rule while failing the philosophy's ambition to put something manageable in the reader's head. Calling every long root a pyramid violation would confuse two different problems.

## What is missing

### Mechanism understanding needs a next step toward judgment

69 makes a useful inference available: clearing a lookup does not retract a promise already held by a caller. 72 separates rejection before acceptance from recovery after accepted work. 74 shows that three occurrences of 250 ms need not be one shared policy field. These are things a reader can get wrong, and the pictures can help correct them.

But the philosophy asks for enough understanding to explain the shape of the change or spot a bad decision. A successful mechanism exercise is a prerequisite, not evidence of that whole outcome. A reader could correctly replay every gesture and still be unable to say why the system retains these references, why a complete policy section crosses a boundary, or which operating conditions would make a retry allowance inappropriate.

There are precedents to build on. [11](../11-chess-coach/README.md) distinguishes the chosen move from alternatives and keeps hypothetical alternatives separate from documented intent. [29](../29-refusal/README.md) separates review questions that should not be made to look like one inevitable move. [35](../35-future-self/README.md) supplies future editing cues. [70](../70-bauhaus-daemon-grammar/README.md) makes a particularly reviewable choice visible: a consumer receives a whole policy section while reading only some leaves, and a scalar override survives the migration. That invites a meaningful question about a wider input versus a minimal projection.

The next observation should therefore include a conditional explanation: “What additional context would make you challenge this choice?” It should accept “the supplied record does not tell me” when that is the honest answer. It should not turn a plausible architectural preference into an invented author rationale. Nor does this require verdict buttons or stored comments; their absence is intentional under rule 2, not a product defect.

### Completeness still tends to reside in a second reading surface

The older large inventories make this obvious: 01 and 14 carry extensive decision lists, 20 enumerates callable contracts, and 21 places a brief orientation above a much larger register. The newer pages have improved the first encounter, but many retain the same division of labor. 62 has fourteen complete-layer records. 69 adds eight readings to the gesture. 77 needs nine readings alongside its films. Test adaptations, deleted cases, public seams, and rationale qualifications often live in those records because the primary metaphor has little to say about them.

This is not an argument to delete those decisions. Rule 8 rightly prevents a spectacular architecture picture from concealing a weakened test. It is an argument to find a better grouping principle than “the mechanism, then everything else.” A test change belongs beside the claim whose evidence it changes. A removed option belongs beside the caller behavior it permits or forbids. A migration exception belongs on the boundary it temporarily crosses.

[52](../52-critique-response-52/README.md) gives unusually useful evidence about the limit of layout work: its compact and fuller pair retain the same thirty propositions and 2,305 words. The reported desktop heights differ, 6,291 versus 7,781 pixels. That is a reduction in scroll distance, not a demonstrated reduction in concepts to retain. [59](../59-critique-response-59/README.md) improves the comparison by correcting misleading arrows, and appropriately leaves the reader-study result absent.

[51](../51-angle-new-subject-51/README.md) remains an important attempt at the actual stack problem: nine fronts organize the many changes around lifetimes and authority. 59's audit is valuable partly because it checks all relevant ancestor fronts before calling a deeper statement new; some alleged omissions disappear under that more careful test, while concrete child-retry, shutdown-budget, and adapter consequences still need parents. This is a better direction than concatenating every good donor, which makes [40](../40-best-of-synthesis/README.md) valuable as reconciliation but costly as an opening.

### Reader evidence has not caught up with representation evidence

“There is no reader evidence” is now too broad. [49](../49-follow-next-49/index.html) records four independent agent-reader sessions on two specific original/revised wording pairs. It is evidence that making those consequences explicit changes answers under those prompts. It is not a human navigation study or a finding that the surrounding page teaches the whole subject.

The serial fresh-reader reports are useful records of what an agent retained and where it descended. They are not independent replications of a human-learning result. Reading earlier reports, or many variants of the same PR in sequence, changes the reader. I have the same limitation here, more strongly: this critique deliberately reads the accumulated criticism before synthesizing it.

52 and 59 prepare comparisons without reporting completed reader results. 56 preserves a partial protocol after interruption. Most new READMEs propose a later comparison with a plain diagram. Repeating that next step does not execute it. The campaign now has enough concrete operations to choose a small test with a discriminating question, rather than funding another general promise to test comprehension later.

The practical missing object is small: claim, revision, source witness, affected artifact locations, corrected wording, and whether that correction has been applied. It can be a local table. It should not become a universal answer key or a score for experiments. The purpose is to keep a known distinction from being flattened again during reuse.

### A shared visual coordinate can quietly overstate comparability

[64's “Jump to the split” state](operations/64-split.png) is a concrete example I operated. The shared playhead is at 7/15 in both builds. The base readout says backend release has settled while graph release remains pending, with the definition cache empty. The head readout says a new promise has been stored, with backend and graph both pending. Those are different selected events in the two recorded sequences.

The page explicitly says equal-numbered columns do not assert simultaneous execution. This is an honest local limit, and the records are not thereby false. However, the prominent paired cache readout can suggest a changed refill behavior when the intended comparison is the release barrier. An observer must undo the visual pairing to interpret it safely.

Preserve the raw scores, but offer semantic checkpoints for comparison: after both clears and before a new query; after both new queries; while project cleanup is held; after it settles or rejects. The separate event orders can remain in deeper evidence. The sustained notes would then teach the outstanding return relationship without making ordinal alignment carry an unintended second claim. I inspected the silent visual representation; I did not evaluate the audio's perceptual effect.

The same discipline applies to measurements. 67's direct-reader terrain measures selected field accesses, not the amount of damage a change could cause. Its fault strip helps expose consequences that reader counts miss, including parent-side validation before worker construction. 71 deliberately uses physical path churn with rename detection off; its tall buildings cannot be read as runtime cost or canonical PR-size rankings. 77's direct-import overlays are not test coverage, and its counterpart normalization need not produce the same counts as 18. 73's selected-role marks are not measured system pressure. These distinctions are often disclosed; the question is whether the picture continues to suggest the stronger interpretation after the reader has read the disclaimer.

### Several criticisms now repeat without changing the next experiment

The refresh chain repeatedly identifies the long middle, surprises on descent, optional-policy credentials, adapter changes, and missing reader comparisons. Some repetition is justified: the artifacts and their descendants still contain the problems. But another large census is not another independent observation of every problem, and a repeated “try readers next” is not progress toward that trial.

The useful turns in this chain are interventions or sharpened distinctions: 48 gives 49 two specific wording targets; 49 changes those targets; 52 freezes content to test layout; 59 repairs the stimulus and withdraws overbroad omission claims. This critique should be judged by whether its narrower correction and comparison targets can change the next run, not by the number of previous critiques it agrees with.

The interrupted entries also need honest accounting. 56, 58, and 60 are finished negative-result handoffs because the workers were stopped and that state was documented. They are neither missing pages to grade nor evidence that their planned representations cannot work. The known result is interruption before the intended artifact or study was complete.

## What I would ask the next work to deliver

**First, carry the small corrections forward.** Use the four-row routing table above when reusing those claims or comparison pages. Keep the exact distinction and source revision. Do not silently modify frozen experimental stimuli, and do not manufacture reasons to eliminate an “unexplained” label. This critique leaves the reviewed artifacts unchanged.

**Second, run one bounded learning comparison.** A suitable candidate is 74's distinction between one shared field and three equal-valued fields, with a conventional view containing exactly the same facts and rationale. Before source access, ask an unfamiliar human to predict which consumers change when one field changes. Then ask what would justify keeping the wider input. Record the explanation, the route taken, and what information was missing. Keep factual prediction and design reasoning distinct; do not score architectural agreement as correctness. A wrong answer should identify a candidate representation failure, not produce an experiment leaderboard. 49 supplies a precedent for a small intervention; it does not supply the human result.

**Third, test composition rather than another independent costume.** Keep 51's stack fronts and try one repeated relationship from 76: what persists, what is replaced, and what a boundary now owns. One complete front should name the relevant behavior, reason or rationale gap, and evidence-changing test decision. A reader should be able to descend into either concrete lifecycle and return without learning an undisclosed parent decision. Freeze the scope and source snapshot before comparing forms. The useful result would be a worked example of composition, including the facts the shared representation could not honestly absorb.

These are small follow-ups because the collection is already large. More representations may still be useful, but the next uncertainty is specific enough to observe: whether a person can use one of these pictures to make a correct prediction and a justified, context-sensitive challenge without rebuilding the missing explanation from the diff.
