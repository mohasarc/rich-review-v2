Every finished folder received an individual handoff and primary-entry reading. README reads concentrated on entry, kind, subjects, declared shape and reuse, attempts, limitations, and next work; many were excerpts rather than full reads of every reproduction appendix. For pages, I read rendered openings and structure, their scope/complete-layer declarations, and selected later decision material. The resulting eighty-one authored rows are in [ledger-notes.json](ledger-notes.json), rendered by [build-ledger.py](build-ledger.py).

[Initial capture manifest](reading/manifest.json), [80's capture](reading/manifest-selected.json). This is an availability observation, not a readability, accessibility, semantic-completeness, or learning certification.

Companions included 31's method and run record, 32's kit guide, 33's second opening, 49's four original/revised copies, 52's density pair, and 59's repaired pair. Additional readings included 31's runbook, 32's authoring contract, 33's pair contract, 49's protocol, 50's companion critique, 52's findings/transfer/empty reader slots, 56's partial protocol, 58's brief, and 60's partial generators. I read 21's full narration transcript; I did not listen to or evaluate its audio.

The Markdown critiques and reader reports were read for their framing, complete-finding summaries, methods, and selected specific disputes. I read 81's main critique in full. For late-arriving 80, I read its complete README and brief, rendered top, all ten authored reading records, and selected opened source readers. Earlier findings supplied leads, not automatic confirmations. The final document explicitly distinguishes rechecked current statements from historical reader reports whose routes I did not repeat.

I did **not** exhaustively read every copied source bank, inspect every hidden room, operate every scenario, or rerun the authors' Symnav tests/probes. Automatically saving all body text does not establish that all that text was read. DOM text can also expose off-camera content in a zoom interface, so it is not a substitute for a visual reading of that state. Selected screenshots were inspected directly where a spatial claim mattered.

This is a primed cumulative reading. The repeated PRs teach their vocabulary to the critic, and earlier critiques disclose their findings. No blind seal, independent human result, or new agent-reader trial is claimed. No sub-agents were spawned.

## Application of the hard rules

Rules 4, 7, 8, 9, and 10 supply the main explanatory tests: complete parents, real boundaries, decisions/reasons, retained context across depth, and a picture that replaces redundant reading. A long root is not automatically a rule-4 violation. An omitted fact in a critic's recollection is not automatically absent from the artifact. A narrower later brief changes the decision scope being evaluated.

19's own explicit account says its no-bodies method cannot fully cover weakened tests and implementation choices. That is a useful negative limit on original rule 8. 20's #127 five-decision layer supplies callable contracts but no characterization-test decision account. Neither observation is a claim that all their source material is missing or incorrect.

Rules 2 and 3 prohibit verdict/comment collection and code-correctness judgment. The operated controls change demonstrations, not stored review judgments. The deliberate absence of review-response collection is not counted as a defect. I did not perform an exhaustive storage/API audit of every script.

Rules 11, 13, and 14 are supported by the entry checks and README census. Readable entry points and declared methods are prerequisites; they are not evidence of learning. [verification.json](verification.json) checks this handoff, not the semantic truth of the whole collection.

There is one newly included operational exception worth recording under rule 12: 79's README and [incident record](../79-failure-domain-pinball/evidence/decision-coverage.md#workspace-incident) report a temporary untracked stylesheet mistakenly written into a daemon worktree, then removed. That is outside the stated build-output exception even though it was reversed. It is a self-reported transient violation, not a finding of remaining source damage. This critique made no writes in either Symnav worktree.

## Tooling and abandoned approaches

The capture/operation harnesses were adapted from 81, which credits 54's capture approach. All new writes are inside this experiment. Playwright was reused from an existing local installation; Chrome ran headlessly at 1440 × 1000 with reduced motion. Initial captures used an ephemeral loopback server, with external requests blocked. Targeted operations opened local files, also blocking HTTP(S). No new dependencies or external research were needed.

Broad capture output proved too bulky for reading. I switched to bounded handoff/opening excerpts and targeted full passages, retaining the captures as receipts. I rejected a novelty ranking and the idea that every new narrow artifact must restate all 26 PRs. No page prototype was attempted.

Two source lookup mistakes were corrected: an initial project-graph path omitted its `workspace/` directory; an excerpt request extended beyond the last line of the pinned policy file. Neither produced a source finding. The first 37 selector searched for `Evidence` while its accessible name is `Evidence depth`; the corrected route uses the observed names. These are harness errors, not failures of the artifacts.

The temporary browsers and local capture server close in `finally` blocks. The final worktree status check is read-only. Queue state, root index, other experiments, and Symnav branches are left to the orchestrator.
