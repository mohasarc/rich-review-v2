# critique-refresh-10

The finished experiments make ownership changes and source evidence much easier to find. They have not yet demonstrated that a human can understand the stack without consuming another large inventory. The most useful next work is to compress decisions without concealing them, attach reasons to the exact choices they explain, and observe a reader making a prediction from the artifact.

This refresh covers **all 38 finished experiments, 01–38**, at **2026-09-13 03:45:06 UTC**: 37 prototypes and the preceding critique. Experiment 36 finished during the main pass; 38 finished during handoff and was then read in full. Experiments 39, 40, and 42 were still running; their drafts did not inform this critique. “Finished” means a brief in `queue/done/` and an experiment README. The [complete reading ledger](experiment-ledger.md) has one row per finished experiment; [scope.json](scope.json) records the census and README hashes. This is artifact criticism against [philosophy.md](../../philosophy.md) and [playbook §1](../../playbook.md#1-what-every-agent-must-produce-hard-rules), not a correctness review of symnav or a human comprehension study.

**A — Completeness is often being purchased with reading volume.**

The clearest recurring admission appears in the READMEs themselves. [01](../01-unconstrained-a/README.md) exposes 141 recovered decision records and 44 policy records. [14](../14-outsider-persona/README.md) gives an outsider 117 PR choices, 25 additional records, and the policy table. [21](../21-narrated-top/README.md) explicitly concedes that its 90-second film cannot carry the full inventory, and places that inventory beneath it. [03](../03-unconstrained-c/README.md) calls the picture plus 24 cards the complete overview. These are honest treatments of rule 4. Multiple screens are not, by themselves, a violation. They are also not evidence that the philosophy's stopping point has been achieved.

The recurring workaround recreates the long middle in a new location: the human must still read many sentences to discover which distinctions matter together. A map with links is an index until its visible contents express the consequences needed to judge the choices. Moving a list above the mechanism does not make that list easy to understand.

There is also a concrete surprise on descent. At [07's initial `main` view](../07-stack-timeline/index.html), the visible #148 summary says “existing idle timing” remains explicit, and the opening names deferred cache/idle changes. Selecting #148 first supplies the actual decision: idle timing starts at construction and resets at navigation acceptance. Those event choices determine what happens during a long running request; they are more than additional source fidelity. The parent should state the event rule. The [captured transition](reading-captures/targeted-navigation.json) preserves the added lines. The timeline remains valuable for the staging/cutover distinction; its global stopping surface needs more than topic names.

The useful comparison is between three costs, not three winners: [13](../13-owner-persona/index.html) spends words on consequences in compact rows; [23](../23-zoom-canvas/index.html) fits 25 choices into six spatial rooms but asks the reader to scan a dense outline; [34](../34-inverted-for-experts/index.html) lets a maintainer recognize a concrete value/test pattern before opening an explanation. None has a human result establishing the better tradeoff. A next compression trial should hold the underlying decision set constant and preserve every independent consequence at the parent, while changing how relationships are drawn.

**B — “Stated” and “unexplained” need a smaller unit than a card.**

The corpus sometimes disagrees because two authors grouped different propositions into one decision. That is not automatically an error. It does make counts of unexplained cards unsuitable for comparison.

| Cross-experiment witness | What the source or artifact actually establishes | Repair to the explanation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| #127 release in 31/33/37 versus the combined release cards in 10/24/32 | The [PR body](source-checks/pr-127-body.md) explicitly names an awaited backend boundary, changes the public return type, and gives a reason for **clearing before** project release. The commit list also contains “Specify awaited semantic resource release.” A reason reconciling changed completion/rejection visibility with the broader preservation promise is a separate question. 32 already says that reconciliation is unexplained inside a card labeled stated. | Keep “declared change,” “reason for clearing order,” and “unexplained reconciliation” distinguishable at the surface. Do not convert a mixed card into one clean status or describe the awaited barrier itself as undisclosed. |
| #148 `policy-testing` retirement in 23 D03 and 35 D03 versus 06 K13 | The [policy document at the actual #148 head](source-checks/pr-148-daemon-policy.md#migration-access) explicitly schedules removal after app-owned mechanism tests move package-local. 06 recovers this; 23 and 35 say no retirement/timing rationale was found in the inspected plans. | The retirement has documented migration context. Keep any genuinely separate question about a particular removed assertion separate. Recheck the pinned plan before presenting absence as a finding. The final stack's rewritten policy document is not a substitute for this historical one. |
| #148 portability in 23 D22 versus 03 choice 02 | 23's title is CRLF normalization, but its missing-reason text asks why this digest mechanism was chosen; the same text acknowledges commit subjects about portability. [The commit record](source-checks/pr-148-commits.json) contains “Specify portable daemon compatibility hashing.” | Separate line-ending portability from the choice of a whole-tree digest and its exemptions. The reason status must follow the particular choice named, rather than a broader unanswered question. |

This is a practical failure mode for the philosophy's most useful sentence, “nobody said.” A false absence sends the human to recover a reason already present. A reason borrowed from a neighboring choice makes the human miss the question that remains. [31's runbook](../31-method-runbook/RUNBOOK.md) states the right rule: split choices with independently meaningful consequences or different rationale. [37](../37-be-weird-b/index.html#await/shape) makes that distinction navigable without losing the cache-clearing context.

**C — The valuable variation changes what can be seen.**

Much of the corpus independently converges on the same reading sequence: ownership picture → complete decision cards → a few mechanisms → source viewer. The recurrent workshop, drawers, trays, and clearing metaphors in 01, 14, 21, 22, 26, 33, and 37 provide familiar orientation, but they are a narrow family of intuitions. Detective, future-self, adversarial, and chess framing often change the introduction more than the underlying representation. The per-experiment ledger records what actually differs.

Several pieces earn their extra representation:

| Piece to inspect | Relationship the representation carries | Limit to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [05's paired maps](../05-boxes-static-vs-runtime/screenshots/01-maps.png) | Import direction and runtime value flow have different arrows and enclosures. A worker sits inside the daemon process; a package does not become a service. | The dense static routes still require scanning. Keep the shared subject across the two maps; do not collapse their meanings. |
| [30's delivery gates](../30-adjacent-pair/index.html) | Completing the worker or the first attachment does not free the next queued request. The current delivery promise and then a resource sample do. ACK and retained result bytes have another lifetime. | This is an illustrative model. In my exercised path, B became available after the sample while A remained unacknowledged. It is not a daemon execution result. |
| [36's contact sheet](../36-be-weird-a/index.html) | Identity reuse becomes a block pattern. Switching from stored reference locations to public service returns removes sharing even though the cached locations remain reused. | The matrix is a bounded observation relation within each build. It cannot express the release-completion difference, so the separate completion readout is necessary. |
| [37's reading loom](../37-be-weird-b/index.html) | The reader can compare two decisions at the same depth, then deepen the selected decision. I followed `clear/mechanism → await/mechanism → await/evidence → await/shape`. | The selected summary repeats in the pane, and the spatial advantage becomes weaker on a phone. Compare it with ordinary tabs using the same content. |
| [07's last two frames](../07-stack-timeline/index.html#pr-148) and [18's missing connection](../18-negative-space/index.html) | A package can contain the new implementation while the shipped consumer remains connected to old copies. | Do not mistake an unchanged dispatcher, a frozen copy, normalized textual equality, and behavioral preservation for the same observation. |

These suggest a selection rule for representations: retain a second view when it exposes a different relationship or supports a different reader action. A generic three-step strip that restates a paragraph does not earn its place merely because it is a diagram. Repeated autoplay is similarly weak: 07 and 10 independently recommend dropping it, while selection and stepping carry their explanations.

**D — The architecture still needs a human question.**

Many pages explain that a responsibility moved because the spec assigned it a new owner. That is useful provenance, but it can stop before the judgment the philosophy wants: what does this boundary make easier, what does it commit us to, and under what future requirement would another cut be better?

[11](../11-chess-coach/index.html) is strongest where alternatives have a concrete consequence: caller purpose selects a timeout, or a fixture translation changes whether bytes touch a file. Its own README identifies generic alternatives such as “one timer for everything” as weaker. [16](../16-drag-the-box/index.html) carries actual imports with a hypothetical module move, so moving toward a leaf package exposes dependencies that still need an owner. Its model expressly stops short of compilation. [29](../29-refusal/index.html) separates clock ownership, process authority, package staging/test ownership, and the host contract into four discussable units; those units are not four validated patches. [35](../35-future-self/index.html) connects a future task to the relevant old decision.

The missing experiment is a small graph of **decisions that constrain other decisions**, with a concrete future request to reason about. For this subject, examples include a host that needs a different output-storage policy, or a different idle event boundary. The author should label the future request hypothetical, show which existing choices it touches, and leave missing product context with the human. That would test judgment rather than another explanation of the same relocation.

**E — Honest evidence is necessary; exhaustive packaging is not the learning result.**

[19](../19-type-level-only/index.html#blind-area) is an instructive negative result. It explicitly says no assertion bodies were inspected and that a no-bodies method cannot fully meet the every-decision rule. Its type observations remain useful, especially structural assignability versus value provenance. A generic “unseen changes” notice does not satisfy rule 8 by surfacing the particular changed error selection or weakened fixture. Treat it as a partial lens or pair it with a separately attributed implementation/test account; do not promote it as a complete review on the strength of its declaration census.

[27](../27-test-honesty/index.html) gives a stronger way to explain test changes: setup → exercised implementation → assertion → what remains outside the observation. [08](../08-adversarial/index.html) adds counterevidence to an adverse interpretation. These explain the architectural meaning of a test migration without turning deleted titles into a coverage verdict. [17](../17-executable-before-after/README.md) carefully names real implementation boundaries and injected inputs. Its live mode was not rerun for this critique. [28](../28-blast-radius-tool/README.md) preserves the failed policy-field reference query and the limits of replacement seeds; its measured zero for error provenance is particularly useful evidence that direct reference reach cannot stand in for importance.

Full source mirrors, exact links, no-server opening, and return navigation are substantial practical achievements across the corpus. Their usefulness is mostly below the stopping layer. The repeated in-page file inventories compete with teaching; many authors independently nominate them for removal from the main path. Likewise, [31](../31-method-runbook/RUNBOOK.md) and [32](../32-kit/KIT.md) correctly warn that line assignments and matching parent/child IDs do not prove semantic completeness. Their methods should preserve those warnings when reused.

Read-only v1 is deliberate. The philosophy's eventual reader response is deferred by rule 2, not forgotten by these experiments. Temporary sketch marks, illustrative game actions, and fixture inputs are compatible with that boundary. Adding approval controls or comment storage would not repair the learning gap. I found no such collection flow in the reviewed entry surfaces; I did not perform a security audit of every script.

**F — The next evidence should come from a reader.**

The corpus contains many tests of the page and the program, but no completed unfamiliar-human comprehension result was reported in the material reviewed. This critic is an agent who has now read the same facts repeatedly; my ability to explain them cannot validate the pages. The unfinished fresh-reader briefs were excluded, and an agent recall exercise would still be distinct from human evidence.

The existing pieces support a bounded next round without a scoring system or a gold answer key:

| Next trial | What stays fixed | What a human would do | What would change the next artifact |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Compress one complete decision surface | Same reviewed choices, reasons, and source scope. | Explain which boundaries moved, then name a consequence without opening detail. | A surprise on descent repairs its parent; a fact repeated without benefit loses duplicate treatment. |
| Use [33's controlled openings](../33-pair-one-variable/pair-contract.md) | Shared body and layout outside the opening. | Different unfamiliar readers start with different openings and predict release completion. | Observe specific misconceptions and requests for detail. Reading both versions sequentially is not an independent comparison. |
| Compare [30](../30-adjacent-pair/index.html) or [36](../36-be-weird-a/index.html) with a static explanation | Same scenario and evidence. | Predict the next permitted event, or identify which observations share an object. | Retain the interaction only if it helps explain the relationship; do not add a correctness score to the review. |
| Transfer [31's method](../31-method-runbook/RUNBOOK.md) or [32's kit](../32-kit/KIT.md) | Frozen method/components, a different operator or the wider #131 subject. | Produce and read another complete explanation. | Record where extraction, rationale attribution, geometry, or navigation needs a different approach. One #127 demonstration has not established reuse. |

The detailed ledger below this critique is for choosing what to inspect, not ranking whole experiments. The useful inheritance is a set of specific mechanisms, distinctions, and failures. Copying every promising piece into one page would reproduce the reading burden this corpus exposes.
