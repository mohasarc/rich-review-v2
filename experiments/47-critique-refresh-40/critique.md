# Make the change smaller in the reader's head

The collection has become good at making mechanisms inspectable. Its missing capability is **compressing the consequential choices into a model a person can carry away**. The most promising experiments expose relationships that prose inventories obscure: a package move without a process move, a shared stored object behind fresh returned objects, or a completed worker whose turn remains blocked. Preserve those operations. More cards, source mirrors, and borrowed devices do not by themselves improve the explanation.

This covers **all 46 finished experiments, 01–46, at 2026-09-13 04:42:09 UTC**, including the earlier critiques. The [experiment ledger](experiment-ledger.md) accounts for every included artifact; [inspection notes](inspection-notes.md) state the reading extent, direct checks, and limits. Experiment 48 was still running at the cutoff. No page was built. This is a primed editorial reading, not an unfamiliar-human comprehension result.

<a id="stopping"></a>
## A. Compress decisions by preserving their consequences

The tension is real. [Philosophy](../../philosophy.md) wants the reader to grasp the change quickly and stop. [Rule 8](../../playbook.md#1-what-every-agent-must-produce-hard-rules) requires every decision, including tests and unrequested work. Many builders resolve this by putting the complete inventory above the mechanism and calling the inventory the top. That is an honest disclosure of reading cost, but the human still has to assemble the model.

01's stack sheet contains 141 choices and a policy register. 14 teaches an outsider what the product does, then presents 117 recorded PR choices, additional changes, and policy values. 21's film lasts ninety seconds, but explicitly points to a much larger complete map. 22 exposes its contracts before the games, then supplies the historical sheet. None should be failed merely for extending below the first screen: rule 4 specifies no viewport limit, and a labelled orientation can precede the complete layer. The unresolved philosophical question is how much synthesis the reader must perform at that layer.

The stack presentations also carry two editorial organizations: the implementing author's historical PR decisions and the explainer's extra findings. Both deserve preservation, but historical enumeration is not yet a net-change explanation. A final host boundary has a live state, staging history, retained constraints, and deferred alternatives. Naming those relations would let a person understand why the #130 temporary surface, #148 staged package, and #149 cutover appear together. 07 makes chronology useful; 14/21/22 explicitly mark intermediate choices. The next step is to make the final consequence the parent of that history, while retaining each historical decision and its reason at an appropriate fidelity.

A long startup or turn can consume it. | A consequential event rule first appears after descent. Reproduces 41/43. |

The [saved route text](inspection-notes.md#routes) supports these readings. They do not mean every newly named function or exact value below a parent violates rule 4. The test is whether the child changes what the reader can infer about the announced decision.

Here is a concrete editing proposal for **27's adapter parent**, not a replacement for its whole PR account:

> Test adapters translate legacy knobs into valid policy, with different effects. Some spool cases add another byte to cross a positive threshold; the transport helper raises inline capacity to at least a default chunk, so an unchanged cleanup case can stay in memory. The capture comparison uses 32 bytes, and records are split at the chunk cap. The adapter seam has a stated reason; the specific 32-byte replacement has no separately located reason.

That paragraph is longer than a topic title and much shorter than the complete apparatus. It announces the differing premises before the reader sees the second-byte diagram. The deeper layer can identify helpers, formulas, and assertions without reversing the parent. No predecessor was edited for this critique.

This suggests a practical compression operation: join propositions only when the parent preserves their relevant conditions, outcomes, and reason scope. A heading such as “tests changed” is not such a join. Nor is “all other behavior stays” a substitute for reading the changed assertions.

## B. Attach reasons to propositions, not card boundaries

The phrase “nobody said” is central to the philosophy because it identifies where human context is missing. Its reliability depends on a proportionate search and a precise claim. The absence of a line in the diff does not establish the absence of a reason in an unchanged plan.

For #127, these are different propositions:

| Proposition | What the supplied material says |
| --- | --- |
| Clear cached entries before project release. | The PR explicitly gives a reason: old semantics must be unavailable during pending or rejecting cleanup. |
| Await service/graph release through the backend. | The after diagram and public signature explicitly disclose it. |
| Reconcile the expanded caller-visible completion/failure contract with the preservation promise. | No separate reconciliation was found in the inspected body and plan accounts. This is narrower than saying the change was hidden. |

I read the supplied body directly. The [source receipt](reading/source-checks.txt) preserves it. 37's clear/mechanism and await/mechanism routes keep these propositions distinct. 40 carries that distinction into its opening and intent comparison. A single *stated* badge can make the clearing reason appear to explain the new wait; a single *unexplained* badge can make an explicitly disclosed change appear undisclosed.

For #148, the policy document at head `20838f8d`, lines 62–66, schedules removal of `policy-testing` after app-owned mechanism tests move package-local. 06 finds that passage. It qualifies the absence claims in 23 and the grouped record in 35. It does **not** explain every removed lint gate, assertion, or observation. 29 asks about the exact enforcement tradeoff; 43 correctly narrows the proposed correction there. Add the migration context and retain the exact unanswered question, rather than flipping an entire group to *stated*.

44's portable-hashing finding supplies a further example. In 23 D22, the detail already says the commit subjects specify portability and line-ending normalization, then says no further reason for the digest mechanism was found. I read that record and the two empty-body commit entries directly. The *unexplained* badge obscures a known purpose; the detailed text does not deny that purpose. Split portability from choices such as the particular digest construction. Do not replace a broad absence label with an equally broad claim that the exact strategy was explained.

This also limits what can be inferred from different decision counts. One author splits clear order, awaited completion, API shape, and preservation tension; another groups them. Counts of unexplained cards cannot measure either coverage or suspicion. The reusable record should preserve the choice, consequence, source of disclosure, specific reason or bounded absence, and the parent that announces it. The page can still choose its own shape.

## C. A second representation must reveal a second relationship

The recurring grammar is a boundary picture, a visible register, a mechanism panel, and a source drawer. “Clue,” “move,” “memory,” and “ticket” often rename that same scan-and-expand task. The strongest departures change the inference available to the reader.

05 holds related owners in view while changing the meaning of edges: static dependency versus runtime movement, with the worker nested in the daemon process. 16's hypothetical relocation carries explicit imports with the module; repairing an address does not make a forbidden dependency disappear. 30 separates worker completion, the captured latest delivery promise, the resource sample, and acknowledgement. In my operated route, latest delivery and then sampling released B while the first attachment remained blocked and A remained unacknowledged. Its [model state](reading/30-latest-before-first.png) makes the distinct lifetimes visible; it is explicitly an illustration, not a new daemon execution.

36's matrix is a relation between observations, not another owner map. The same cached reference locations can support fresh public projections. Switching to the return lens produces a diagonal matrix. Yet the plot still says “Reference locations,” with “One large block” and “Two smaller blocks” captions beneath it. I reproduced [43's finding](../43-critique-refresh-20/critique.md#pictures) in this [captured state](reading/36-reference-return.png). The displayed data, selected-pair explanation, and surrounding interpretation no longer name the same relation. Correct data and a working toggle are insufficient.

40 improves this by putting cache identity and service-return identity beside each other. Its [position view](reading/40-position-contact.png) states why reused locations coexist with fresh node arrays. It also explicitly says the identity records cannot show the changed cleanup barrier. That limitation justifies a separate release representation: it answers a question the matrix cannot answer. This is the philosophy's multiple-representation requirement made concrete.

The synthesis still has a translation cost. Its natural-request trace uses a three-function fixture; its identity records use a different one-file fixture; its cleanup records use a separate two-file fixture. All are locally labelled and their provenance is useful. The concern is pedagogical, not an accusation of mixing measurements: the reader must repeatedly map different observation names and inputs onto the same contracts. A further composition should either keep one fixture across these views or explain exactly what changes and why the new witness is necessary. Whether that reduces reader effort is unmeasured.

The thirteen-donor requirement is a construction constraint, not evidence that thirteen contributions belong in a finished lesson. 40's strongest contributions are repaired attribution, an early empty-but-pending comparison, paired interfaces, and scoped reuse. Its optional depth matrix is a navigation alternative. The claim-ticket analogy may help a different reader, but its necessity is not established by giving it a donor credit. A useful selection question is: **if this view disappears, which prediction or distinction becomes harder to explain?** That keeps multiple representations without giving every representation equal prominence.

## D. Let evidence challenge the inventory

39/42 identify concrete disagreements that repetition did not wash out. 01 teaches first configured ownership; 22 says the graph keeps first configured ownership; 14 says last. At #126 head, `buildOwnership` iterates configurations and overwrites the primary map on each match. The last matching configured project becomes primary. This is a source correction, not a deep layer silently changing the story. Likewise, 01's #127 card says one old release call gains `await`. I compared the old test-and-helper tail with the new file: the five existing cases' tail is byte-identical. Six new service cases precede it. [Both checks are recorded](reading/source-checks.txt); I did not rerun the tests.

These errors matter because they appear in supposedly complete surfaces with substantial supporting apparatus. A source archive is valuable only if it can revise the explanation. The broader lesson is not that a different presentation style would have prevented an incorrect fact.

The restricted methods expose complementary blind spots. 19 excludes implementation and assertion bodies and openly cannot meet rule 8 for a whole PR. That disclosure is a useful negative result, not a waiver. 20 enumerates callables and gives unusually clear treatment to #131's new parent-side validation before worker creation; its #127 five-choice top does not account for the added tests. 28's first policy-field query missed known consumers; its replacement seeds and unmeasured decisions are candidly reported. A low or zero direct-reference count cannot rank error provenance or a lost test premise by human importance.

The worker case is particularly instructive. “The worker host reparses policy” names a mechanism. “Malformed policy can throw synchronously before a thread is spawned” teaches a consequence. At #131 head, `fromSerialized` occurs at constructor lines 80–82, before `new Worker` at 86. The [pinned excerpt](reading/source-checks.txt) supports that ordering; the supplied policy validation is a prerequisite for the consequence. I did not execute a malformed-policy case. 20 brings the consequence to its parent; repeated readers miss it in many other accounts.

27's apparatus → exercised code → assertion → limit is worth preserving even where its parent needs repair. It explains why an added default recovery case can also pass on the base, and why a migrated test title does not establish identical observation. 34 puts concrete evidence values at the entrance. These are more useful to judgment than counts alone.

31's hunk assignments and 32's component relationships offer structural checks. They cannot establish that a sentence entails every assigned proposition, that a reason supports the exact choice, or that a reader understood it. Preserve the receipts at evidence depth; do not make the receipt inventory the reader's curriculum.

<a id="judgment"></a>
## E. Reach the choice the human might reconsider

Many explanations accurately answer where a responsibility went. A plan quotation can explain why the author followed that direction. The philosophy goes further: the human should be able to identify an undesirable choice and supply context the agent lacks. That requires a connection between the mechanism and a requirement that could make another choice preferable.

11 is most useful when its alternative changes something concrete, such as a fixture's ability to cross a storage threshold. Generic alternatives that obviously duplicate a decision invite agreement without requiring much thought. 16 makes the cost of a hypothetical move tangible. 29 separates clocks, authority, staging, and client behavior into questions that could receive different answers, while honestly declining to claim four buildable cherry-picks. 35 creates entrances from a future edit to the relevant historical decision; accurate reason attribution becomes especially important when the page acts as memory.

A missing representation is the dependency between **decisions**: which choices follow from another boundary, which are independently reversible, and which rely on an unstated product assumption. This direction was already proposed by 41; it remains untested. On these subjects, an explicitly hypothetical host with different output-storage needs could expose the relation between optional facade policy, required mechanism slices, package-owned capture, and the test seams. The artifact should distinguish source-stated constraints from the reader's supplied requirement and leave the unexplained tradeoff open. It should not invent the implementing author's motive.

This is not a request to add approval buttons. Rule 2 intentionally postpones the response product described by the philosophy. The current absence of stored comments or verdicts is compliance. Ephemeral sketching, prediction, and manipulating an illustrative model can support understanding inside the read-only scope.

<a id="learning"></a>
## F. Change the next observation, not just the next wrapper

39, 42, 44, and 46 seal page-derived accounts before their independent source pass. That is useful evidence about omissions, recall, and false claims. Their serial exposure to the same small set of subjects makes them unsuitable for deciding which opening teaches a novice better. This critique has read those reports and the pages; it is even more primed. More agreement among us does not fill the unfamiliar-human gap.

13 and 14 establish different persona entrances on different scopes; they do not demonstrate the philosophy's stronger claim that an owner and newcomer can use the same document at different depths. 33 holds a shared body and opening length fixed, but its own pair contract acknowledges changes in emphasis. 25 permits reconstruction, but its supplied labels can cue recognition. 31/32 demonstrate a method and kit on #127; another operator applying them unchanged to #131 or #148 would reveal different constraints. None of these is a failure for lacking a user study. They are apparatus whose intended benefit remains unobserved.

I would pursue three bounded pieces of work, using the current corpus:

| --- | --- | --- |
| Repair and compare a compact complete layer with a fuller register. | The same reconciled choices, test premises, rationale scope, and evidence exits. 13/23/34 offer distinct compression ideas. | Before descent, an unfamiliar reader explains the change and identifies uncertain choices. After descent, record the exact proposition that revises the account. No score or hidden gold list. |
| Examine the parts of 40 separately. | Its corrected #127 claims and scoped evidence. Keep the release comparison and identity relationship available; vary optional explanation/entrance devices. | Which unshown case can the reader predict, and which representation do they cite? | Transfer 31 or 32 beyond the existing demonstration. | The documented procedure or components before adapting them. | Where extraction, reason granularity, geometry, or parentage fails on a wider subject. Record the required changes; successful rerendering of the same ledger is insufficient. |

The follow-up should retain the actual reader explanation and its revisions as research observations outside the review interface. It need not collect a verdict or build a scoring system. The morning choice is which missing observation to obtain. Another refresh that simply restates this table would add little.
