## 2. The change in at most three sentences

PR #131 (head b100221) stops CLI-side daemon mechanisms in apps/cli from keeping their own numeric defaults. They now require the frozen daemon policy snapshot from packages/daemon, which already travelled CLI process → daemon process → worker thread, and read output, resource, transport, startup, shutdown, delivery and diagnostic limits from it. Consumers of the same section keep distinct purposes: status 100 ms vs ordinary 250 ms timeouts, one reattachment per request vs one fetch-resume per attempt, and separate lifecycle and diagnostic slices. A duplicate CLI resource-policy class and its tests are deleted, legacy numeric test knobs move into seven Test* adapters, a source denylist grows to 36 retired names, and the fixed second startup launch becomes a policy-counted child-failure retry loop, with some unexplained leftovers and behavior edges flagged.

## 3. Boxes and boundaries

Units:
- **packages/daemon: "ONE POLICY"**. The existing frozen snapshot, "same values across processes". It has seven sections (materials/colours): output (pink), resources (green), transport (blue), startup (orange), shutdown (teal), diagnostics (purple), delivery (amber). Package-side policy validation exists ("Policy validation requires positive, ordered output caps"), and so do package policy tests ("Existing package tests overlap").
- **apps/cli: "MECHANISM OWNERS STAY HERE"**. The consumers, in three families:
  - I / Capacity (pointed pane): Capture & spool (OUT, "same raw caps"), Control framing (NET, "control cap"), Worker chunks (OUT, "chunk cap", crack 09), Resource supervisor (MEM, "derived once", crack 04).
  - II / Purpose (four-lobed pane): Status observer (NET, 100 ms), Ordinary exchange (NET, 250 ms), Reattach request (GET, "1 / request"), Resume result fetch (GET, "1 / attempt", crack 08). Records name "the same transport class" for both timeouts.
  - III / Lifecycle (round pane): Startup coordination (UP, "child retry: 1"), Lifetime & stopping (DOWN, "own deadlines"), Logs & traces (LOG, "own retention").
  - Named pieces in the records: WorkspaceDaemon, the "duplicate CLI resource-policy class" (deleted), StoredCommandOutput, "Registry grace", "worker parent", the codecs, and "The transport adapter".
- **Runtime boundaries: CLI process → daemon process → worker thread.** "Those boundaries stay." The process and worker snapshot bridges "already existed".
- **Tests:** CLI tests, seven new Test* helpers (adapters), a meta-test with a production-source denylist, package tests, and benchmark records.

Which boundary moved, and how:
- **Threshold authority moved.** Numbers used to come from consumer-local defaults and optional knobs inside apps/cli modules ("Before: hatching and open lead show local knobs"). Now principal consumers take a required policy input whose values come from the packages/daemon snapshot ("Heavy perimeter: required policy input at principal consumers"). The mechanisms themselves stay in apps/cli, and the process and worker boundaries do not move.
- **The resource derivation boundary collapsed to one place.** The CLI duplicate resource-policy class and WorkspaceDaemon's fallback derivation are deleted, so resources are "derived once".
- **The test tuning boundary moved.** Tests no longer tune thresholds through runtime/production knobs. Test* adapters build policy-backed instances, and production compatibility overloads are avoided.
- **Signatures widened.** Output limits "now travel through storage and codec signatures too".
- **Not fully uniform** (record 09): consumers accept whole snapshots, sections/Picks or bare numbers. Some optional or per-call seams survive, and the worker parent re-parses serialized policy.

## 5. Questions the top layer left open

1. What is "composition" concretely? Where is each consumer handed the policy (a composition root, command registration), and who picks "status-observer" vs ordinary?
2. Which consumers are "principal consumers", and which non-principal ones still take optional numbers (besides the record-09 seams)?
3. Did any production numeric value change? For example, were the old capture/spool constants, control caps and lifecycle timers equal to the policy defaults? The page implies preservation but gives no per-value list.
4. Record 08: is the error change (later completion error vs first accepted-close error) user-visible? Does anything branch on the error kind? Is it better or worse?
5. Record 06: is the 5 s execute admission bound a policy value or a literal? Did it change?
6. Record 07: what exactly were the "fixed retry structure and a boolean" before?
7. Record 13: which 36 names, how many are new, and what kind of bypass would the guard miss?
8. Record 04: what are "policy circuit fields" in benchmark records, and do they change benchmark output consumers?
9. Record 11: after this change, does any test still force an actual spill to disk?
10. Record 12: what behavior did the 256-MiB derivation row cover that the 1-byte boundary does not?
11. Record 14: which failures count as "child failures"? What bounds a hung (non-progressing) startup if there is no healthy-startup deadline?
12. Record 05: "min(maximum, floor(stop/2))": maximum of what? And what is "drain ACK polling"?
13. Record 09: what does "Registry grace" guard, who uses the per-call override, and why does the worker parent re-parse policy instead of receiving a leaf?
14. What did the "Before" window (hatching, open lead, local knobs) actually show?
15. The window shows cracks only for 04, 08 and 09, but records 11 and 12 also carry the fractured-number badge. Are the test-only gaps deliberately not on the window?
