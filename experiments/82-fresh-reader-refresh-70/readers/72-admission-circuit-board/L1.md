## 2. The change in at most three sentences

The client-side machinery that sends one workspace command to the symnav daemon (route choice, admission handling, socket transport, delivery recovery) moves out of `apps/cli` into the `@symnav/daemon` package: #148 stages the package, #149 switches the shipped CLI to `DaemonClient`, leaving the CLI with argv parsing and workspace discovery. Along the way transport is split into framing / validation / socket-I/O owners that track three submission states, and local fallback is only allowed with proof the daemon did not take the work (never submitted, or an authenticated, self-consistent retryable rejection — the wire retrySafe bit is no longer trusted on its own, a tightening credited to #134). Once the daemon accepts a request the caller can only recover delivery (one result-fetch per attempt, one reattachment per request) or report a controlled failure; it never re-runs the command locally.

Units the top layer names:

| D2 DELIVERED "daemon result" (C) / D3 CONTROLLED "failure · no local replay" (C) | caller | the two non-local outcomes |
| transport internals (reading 04) | caller | framing, validation, socket I/O — "separate owners" |

Boundaries that moved:
- **Package/ownership boundary** (the headline move): main "keeps this circuit in apps/cli"; #148 "stages the daemon package" (package exists, not yet used); #149 "connects the shipped CLI to DaemonClient". The CLI/daemon-package line now runs between argv + workspace discovery (CLI) and routing + admission + transport + recovery (daemon package). The local run stays host-supplied (H).
- **Transport responsibility boundary**: one transport becomes separate framing / validation / socket-I/O owners (reading 04), explicitly while preserving delivery and authentication classifications.
- **Retry-authority boundary**: permission to replay locally moves from the daemon's wire boolean (trusted on main) to the caller's code table (#134 tightening).
- Unchanged process boundary: caller (C) vs daemon process (D, admission only) vs host (H, local run).

## 5. Questions the top layer left open

1. What does the user get in the "submitted-unconfirmed" case (e.g., silence after the write)? A controlled failure? Can reattach apply, or only after an *authenticated accepted* close?
2. Queue has a state beyond "Accepting"/"draining" (a "Closed" label exists only in a dropdown option): does closed permit fallback?
3. Routing "fallback" (incompatible version record) goes local, but an admission rejection coded "incompatible" does not permit local. Why the asymmetry, and are these the same "incompatible"?
4. "Selected startup runs independently" — who starts the daemon, does the command wait, and what happens if startup fails?
5. What are a "manifest", a "capture" and a "record offset"? When is there no manifest, and what happens then?
6. "Completion has no deadline" — what bounds a hung accepted request? Can the CLI wait forever?
7. What is #134 and where does it sit relative to main → #148 → #149? Is that tightening inside this slice?
8. Besides #134's tightening, is #149 behaviour-identical to main (the page disclaims "whole-stack parity")? Which of the 25 decisions are new in this slice vs carried from main?
9. What makes a rejection "consistent" — is not-ready + retrySafe=false also corrupt?
10. What are "reply coordinates", and what happens on mismatch (corrupt? transport failure?)?
11. What does the caller see on token mismatch disconnect — a transport failure? Does it count as submitted?
12. What happens if ACK fails, or verification fails?
13. What are "inner worker failures" and how do they surface to the user?
14. What does "response-capacity" mean? What does "clean" in "clean fetch exhaustion" mean vs unclean?
15. What changes to user-visible exit codes / messages, if any?
16. Why do the default signal's counters show "execute writes 2 / admission samples 2" — does a reattach go through admission again and hit the "matching duplicate" guard?
17. Naming: "resources" (reading) vs "MEMORY not paused" (board) vs "Resource pause" (control) vs "resource-pressure" (code) vs "controlled-resource" (terminal) — same concept or different?
