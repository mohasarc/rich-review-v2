## What I tried

Offered the same hand movement to two return handles. Base moves freely; head stretches toward an asymptote while cleanup is pending and recoils when released. Completing or failing cleanup frees it. The actual promise-state readout remains authoritative even when the illustrative handle is at rest. Displacement represents neither elapsed time nor measured force; this is a visual/motor analogy, not hardware haptics.

Considered friction-driven erasure before implementation, then discarded it: rubbing implies gradual destruction, while this clear is synchronous and caller-held promises can still settle. No full implementation was abandoned halfway. Eight controlled release runs, plus refresh and old-promise recordings, ground the status readouts without claiming a daemon trace.

The audit corrected an excerpt that initially stopped before the new project await, separated test-selection intent from rationale, and verified resize, touch cancellation, and reduced motion. A final survey script accidentally overwrote experiment 52’s README; I restored its exact original 68 lines from its worker transcript and verified the full original Git blob hash. The [restoration receipt](evidence/census-restoration.json) records the repair. No Symnav source was affected.

## What I would drop

The brass finish is expendable. The six single-letter cache slots are also secondary; they need a legend and may add less than the result-identity readout. Full-file source context is useful for auditing but bulky for ordinary reading.

## What I would do next

Give unfamiliar readers this page and an otherwise identical page with resistance disabled. Ask them to predict querying during pending cleanup and receiving a cleanup failure; preserve their explanations without a score or leaderboard.
